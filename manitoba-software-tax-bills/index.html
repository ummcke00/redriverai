That New 7% on Your Software Bills Is Real — Here's How Not to Overpay It

Since January, a second tax line has been showing up on software invoices across Manitoba. Business owners are used to the 5% GST — they can smell a typo at a glance. So when a bill arrives with another seven percent stacked on top of a subscription that never had provincial tax before, the first reaction is usually: "is this a mistake?"

It isn't. And before you pay it blindly for another year, there are a few things worth knowing — including three legitimate ways the charge shrinks.

What the charge actually is

Effective January 1, 2026, Manitoba expanded its Retail Sales Tax to cover cloud computing. The province's own Bulletin 033 (revised January 2026) lists exactly what got caught: software as a service, platform and infrastructure services, cloud storage, and website hosting. If your business merely accessed software running on someone else's server — which is what nearly every modern subscription is — that charge used to escape Manitoba tax. The gap is closed.

The arithmetic on a familiar bill: a $50/month subscription used to arrive as $52.50 with GST. Now it arrives as $56.00 — $50, plus $2.50 GST, plus $3.50 RST. Every cloud line on every card statement and AP batch in the province went through the same change at once, which is why bookkeepers everywhere spent January asking the same question you're asking.

One group escaped by luck: anyone who prepaid an annual subscription in December 2025, before the rules changed, owes no RST on that service period — even the months that run into 2026. That's why two identical companies can be paying different totals for the same tool this year.

Why some bills jumped by more than 7%

If your software line went up 12% instead of 7%, nothing is broken. GST never applied a discount — it simply sat at 5% — and the new 7% stacks on the same base. Combined, cloud charges in Manitoba now carry 12% tax on top of the list price. On a stack of ten subscriptions, that's real money, which is exactly why the next section matters.

Three legitimate ways the charge shrinks

1. Make vendors split the invoice

This is the one most businesses miss. Under the bulletin, a bundled maintenance or support contract that mixes taxable and exempt services is treated as fully taxable — unless the exempt services are described and shown as separate invoice lines. Training, consulting, data processing, and help-line support can all qualify as exempt when they're itemized rather than folded into the software price.

In practice: ask your vendors to break the subscription out from the services on each invoice. One clean line item can be the difference between 7% on everything and 7% on only the part that's actually taxable. If your accounting person has ten minutes, this is the highest-value ten minutes of their month.

2. Know that custom software is exempt

Software designed and developed solely for one business — and the programming services around it — is exempt from RST under Bulletin 033. Packaged, off-the-shelf software stays taxable. There's a boundary worth respecting: if the same build gets reused or resold to other customers, those copies are taxable, and a program built for many customers in an industry isn't "custom" at all.

For buyers, the takeaway is simple: when you're quoted a custom build, that work shouldn't carry the new tax. If a 7% line appears on genuinely custom development, ask why.

3. Check whether your usage prorates

If software is used both inside and outside Manitoba, the province allows RST to be prorated based on the share of licences or users located here. Teams that span provinces sometimes owe less than the full charge. It's worth a call to the vendor's billing department if your crew isn't entirely in-province.

One surprise to expect, not fight

Remote help on taxable software is itself taxable — if a consultant connects remotely to configure, install, or troubleshoot taxable software, RST applies even when the consultant is outside Manitoba. That one catches people because it looks like a service, not a product. It's still on the bill, and it's correct.

If you're on the selling side

If your business sells SaaS or cloud services to Manitoba customers, the province expects you to register with Manitoba Finance and collect the 7%, regardless of where you operate. And a trap for providers: the cloud services you buy to run your own operation don't count as purchases for resale — the tax applies to what you consume, too. Budget for it.

The fifteen-minute version

Today or Monday morning: pull this year's software and hosting bills, confirm the 7% is being applied (it should be), then pick the three largest vendors and ask each one to itemize exempt services on separate lines. That single habit typically recovers a few points on the services slice — and unlike most expense work, it keeps paying every month after.
