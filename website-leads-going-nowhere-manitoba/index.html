Why Your Website Leads Go Nowhere — and How to Catch Every One

You paid good money for a website. A few people trickle in every week. Some of them probably wanted to hire you.

Did the last one actually reach anybody?

On most small-business sites we look at, the honest answer is "nobody knows" — because contact forms fail quietly. No error on the screen, no angry email from a customer. The inquiry just vanishes, and the owner never finds out until they wonder why "the website doesn't bring work."

We know this happens because it happened to us. This fall we ran a hard test on our own funnel and found five separate breaks between a visitor typing their name and anyone at Red River AI knowing they existed. All five are fixed now. Here's the list, because every one of them is probably on your site too.

The five quiet ways a site loses leads

1. The form submits into the void

Our audit-request form was silently rejecting every single visitor — the backend returned an authorization error to each submission while the page showed a friendly "thanks, we'll be in touch." Weeks of visitors, zero leads received. A form can look perfectly fine from the outside and deliver nothing. The only way to know is to submit one yourself and watch what lands.

2. The "email us" address is dead

A greeting mailbox nobody checks is the same as no mailbox. Ours was dead; inquiries sent there simply disappeared. Whatever address is on your contact page — is it a real inbox a human opens daily? Test it from your phone right now.

3. The automatic reply never sends

When someone requests something — a quote, a report, a callback — speed is the whole game. Our system was supposed to send each person their report automatically, and the credential it needed had gone stale, so nobody ever got anything. The lead filled out a form, waited, heard nothing, and called someone else. If your site promises anything by email, that promise needs a heartbeat check.

4. The fields arrive blank

This one is sneaky: our form was reaching the backend after one fix — but a wiring error wrapped the payload twice, so every record arrived empty. We had "leads" in the system with nothing in them: no name, no phone, no message. A lead you can't call back is not a lead. Blank submissions are the most common failure we see when we audit other people's funnels, and the only symptom is a strange feeling that the website "doesn't work."

5. Nobody gets told a lead arrived

A person submits at 7 PM Friday. Who sees it? If the answer is "whoever happens to check," the answer is nobody until Monday — and a lead that waits three days hires whoever answered first. Every real lead should ping somebody immediately: a text, a chat message, an email that isn't the junk folder. Ours now does; until three days ago, it didn't.

The 30-minute funnel test

You don't need a developer to find out if your site leaks. Do this today:

  1. Open your own website on your phone, on cellular data — not the office Wi-Fi a developer tested it on.
  2. Fill out your own contact or quote form like a real customer. Use a real name and your own cell number.
  3. Start a timer. Whoever should have been alerted — you, your office email, your phone — clock the wait.
  4. Check every promised follow-up actually arrived. Then email your own "info@" address and see if it's alive.

If all five steps came back clean, congratulations — you're in the minority, and the rest of this site has ideas for what to do with a funnel that works. If two or more broke, you just recovered customers you were already paying to attract.

What catching every lead is worth

Do the arithmetic for your own trade. If your site brings in even two extra inquiries a month that currently vanish, and you close one of them at your average job value — multiply that by twelve months. For most trades and shops around the southeast, that's thousands of dollars a year, recovered by fixing plumbing that already exists. No new ad spend. No new website. Just stop losing what already came in.

What a fixed funnel looks like

After our repairs, every inquiry does this:

  • The form fills itself out of the conversation and confirms to the customer instantly — they get their report or answer in under a minute.
  • The owner gets an immediate alert with the details, so a 7 PM Friday lead gets a same-evening reply.
  • A daily automated health check walks the whole path — form, backend, mailbox, alerts — so a silent break gets caught within a day, not after a season of lost customers.

That's the difference between a website that's a brochure and one that's an employee. We build exactly this for shops, haulers, crews and plants across Manitoba — see how it fits trucking, manufacturing, farms, and construction.
