Worried the Free Audit Is a Sales Trap? Here's the Whole 30 Minutes, Up Front

Every business owner has sat through one: the "free consultation" that turns out to be forty minutes of slides and a quote before anyone asked what you actually do. So when a website says free audit, the reasonable reaction is suspicion. Fair. Here is the entire half hour, up front, including the part where we tell you the numbers don't work — because that happens, and pretending otherwise is exactly how those other calls feel.

Minutes 0–5: what's eating you

We ask one question and mostly shut up: what's the one form, report, or chase that eats time every single week? Not a worst-case hypothetical — the thing that actually happened this week. Freight paperwork, inspection sheets, quote follow-ups, records for an auditor, timesheets. You talk; we take notes. No deck gets opened on our side.

If nothing repetitive comes to mind, we'll say so in the first five minutes and give you the half hour back — more on that at the end.

Minutes 5–15: one form, end to end

We pick one document and walk it start to finish: who fills it, on what, what gets retyped afterward, who gets emailed, and what happens when it's late or wrong. This is where the clipboard math shows up.

Our case study started exactly here. Inspect That in La Broquerie was running 450 truck and trailer inspections a month, each costing about 5 minutes of handling — retyping results, building the report, emailing the right people across three locations. Nobody had ever added that up on one page.

Minutes 15–25: the arithmetic, out loud

Minutes times frequency, on a calculator, on the call: 5 minutes × 450 inspections = about 37 hours a month. That's the real Inspect That number, and it's why their reports now generate and email themselves the moment an inspection is submitted — manual compilation has sat at zero since go-live in October.

Then hours times a loaded wage, because a saved hour only matters if it moves to billable or operational work. If the arithmetic lands small — a form that runs 20 times a month at 2 minutes each — we say so on the call. A small number is a real answer, not a failure.

Minutes 25–30: the two exits

Exit one: the numbers work. The next step is a pilot against your real workflow — not a demo. At Inspect That, week one was true inspections, including the failures, which is how a grading path that couldn't submit got fixed before it ever mattered in the field.

Exit two: the numbers don't work. You keep the hour map and the dollar figure anyway. No pilot, no proposal, no follow-up campaign. We've audited our own operation this way — it's how we found five silent lead-breaks on this website: a form rejecting every visitor, a dead mailbox, auto-replies that never sent, blank double-wrapped submissions, and no arrival alert. Some of what an audit finds, you can fix yourself the same afternoon.

What you leave with, either way

Four things, regardless of how the call ends:

1. The hour map — where the time goes, task by task, on one page.
2. The dollar figure behind it.
3. The 30-minute DIY funnel test for your website, so a silent lead-break never eats another month.
4. The invoice tip that recovers points on the new 7% software tax — ask your biggest cloud vendors to itemize exempt services on separate lines.

If you want to know what a build costs before we ever talk, it's on the pricing page — in CAD, before tax, no quote games.

"Will you hound me after?"

One email, if you asked for one — the report link and the numbers we worked through together. No "just circling back" series. A follow-up you didn't ask for is just the pitch wearing a different hat, and it would undercut the whole point of publishing this page.

Who this isn't for

If your operation doesn't have a repetitive form, report, compliance record, or quote chase somewhere in it, the audit will find nothing — and we'd rather tell you that in the first five minutes than sell automation for its own sake. The honest version of this offer is that roughly a third of audits should end at minute five.
