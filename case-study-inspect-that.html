Where they started

Every inspection lived on a clipboard. At the end of the day, someone retyped the results, built the report, and emailed the right people — the barn, head office, the on-call crew. Roughly 450 inspections a month, times the minutes each one costs in handling. When a report went out late or a detail was mis-typed, the fix was another phone call.

What we built

📋

iPad-first inspections

Inspectors work on a tablet: guided zones, PASS / FAIL / RERINSE grading, notes and photos. No paperwork leaves the yard.

📨

Reports that send themselves

The moment an inspection is submitted, the PDF report generates and emails itself to the right recipients per location. No compilation, no "I'll send it tonight."

🧾

Audit trail & amendments

Every submission is logged with error codes and retention, amendments are marked and re-sent, and history stays searchable. Compliance-grade by default.

How it went live

Pilot first, against their real workflow — not a demo. Annette's team ran true inspections, including failures, in week one. We fixed what the field surfaced (a grading path that couldn't submit, display details her inspectors flagged), and it's been in production since October 2026. Iteration didn't stop at launch: their change list drives the next build.
