What drives the cost up (so you can keep it down)

  • Number of systems involved. One form into one inbox is cheap. Five systems that need to talk to each other costs more — which is why we start with the highest-pain single flow.
  • Who touches it. Automations your whole staff relies on get more testing and monitoring. Worth it, and priced accordingly.
  • Security requirements. Customer data means the build needs proper handling. We do security-first by default — our founder spent 18 years at Steinbach Credit Union in programming, SQL reporting, and network & cyber security.

How to know if you're even ready

You're ready if any of these sound like your week: paper forms that get typed into a computer later; quotes and estimates that go out days late because someone's busy; follow-ups that live in someone's head; reports assembled by hand from three spreadsheets. If two or more of those are true, automation will pay for itself inside a quarter.
