Every AI Automation Guide We've Written for Manitoba Business

Thirteen guides, four industry pages, one case study, and a pricing page — every one written for the same reader: a Manitoba owner-operator whose operation runs fine except for the paperwork, the chasing, and the rekeying around the edges. Each guide does the same three things: shows where the hours actually go, does the arithmetic with your own numbers instead of industry averages, and includes a 15-minute test you can run this week before believing any of it. Pick your problem below.

Pick your paperwork problem

  • Where AI automation actually saves hours — the overview: where the retyping, chasing and compliance hours go in a typical week, with the La Broquerie inspection numbers (450 reports a month, 37 hours back) as the worked example.
  • Quotes that never got followed up — the quote ledger nobody keeps, the three quiet ways follow-up dies, and the day-3/7/14 ladder that runs itself and stops the moment a customer replies.
  • Work orders on paper — the ticket that can't be found mid-job, the invoice retype where the extras vanish, and the change order that lived as a handshake.
  • Inventory and lot tracking — why a count is a photograph of the past, what happens when the auditor asks for last Tuesday's lot journey, and the trail that builds itself from receiving forward.
  • Scheduling by whiteboard — the board that lives in one head, the gut-feel double-booking, the dead windshield runs, and the shared calendar that survives the board-holder's day off.
  • Missed calls — after-hours voicemails, busy lines mid-job, counter handoffs that lose the details: every one a job you never quoted.
  • Timesheets and payroll — capture-by-memory rounds to whole numbers, the retype filters out the call-outs, and Friday close becomes a dispute over pocket-notebook hours.
  • Freight POD paperwork — BOLs, signed PODs riding in door pockets, per-town billing rules, cross-terminal matching, and the held invoices waiting on clean paper.
  • Shop-floor job travelers — the traveler as a routing slip instead of a record, the status hunt mid-job, and the close-out scatter that delays invoicing.
